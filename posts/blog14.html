My Life from a Distance: Embracing Solitude and Code

Published on April 15, 2026

For as long as I can remember, people have called me "different." Ten years ago, I was a reserved kid who preferred the company of a computer screen over a crowd. Today, I am essentially that same person. While the world seems to value being loud and constantly connected, I’ve found that my greatest peace comes from watching life from a distance.

The Ghost of the Lunch Box

Back in my school and college days, I had a habit that people found weird: I couldn't eat in front of others. I would carry my lunch every day, but I was too shy to even open the box. I felt like everyone was watching, judging, or waiting for a reason to laugh. People did laugh, sometimes. They saw me as "different," and for a long time, I let that silence define me. But looking back, that wasn't a weakness—it was just the early signs of a person who values their internal world more than social performance.

"I love being alone and watching people from a distance. I really love it."

Why I Choose the Laptop Over the Office

Recently, I had the chance to take a job in the BPO sector. On paper, it was a job, but I knew it wasn't my path. The idea of forced conversations and constant noise felt like a direct attack on the peace I’ve built. Instead, I choose to spend my time building. Whether it’s playing kirka.io or working on Octoview—my current project using the Octokit API to manage GitHub—I feel most alive when my ideas are coming into reality on a screen.

I’m not trying to be a "great" coder for the sake of fame. I just love building basic applications that solve my own problems. It's my way of thinking.

Small Worlds, Deep Love

I don’t feel the need for a partner to hold hands with for the rest of my life. My world is small, but it is full. It’s full of my cats—whom I love feeding and caring for—and it’s full of the deep respect I have for my parents. I often wish for my mom and dad to reach Jannah, as they are the ones who have supported this "different" version of me from the start.

So, if you see someone watching from the sidelines or choosing to stay home with their cats and their code, don't feel bad for them. We aren't lonely; we are just finally home.
