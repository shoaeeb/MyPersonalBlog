Trying to Understand My Own Mind

Published on May 2026

What hurts me the most these days is slowly realizing that I may not be able to handle a normal full-time job the way other people can. I had opportunities to work in BPO and even sales jobs in Saudi Arabia, but I didn’t take them. Part of me still wonders if I made the wrong decision, while another part knows I was already struggling mentally and emotionally.

Living with a serious mental health condition changes the way you experience life. During my final year of college, even basic things felt difficult to navigate. My mind was constantly heavy, confused, and exhausted. At times, I even feel paranoid that people—including my doctor—might secretly be laughing at my condition, even though I know that may not actually be true.

One incident during college affected me deeply. I had texted a girl a few times, and she accused me of harassment. After that, it felt like my entire identity became reduced to that one moment. I felt judged, embarrassed, and exposed, as if everyone around me only saw me as “that person.” The strange thing is, I stopped thinking about her long ago. But the shame and confusion from that experience stayed with me.

I wish I could go back and experience life with a healthier mind and better clarity.

Now, when I look at my life, I often feel scared about the future. My batchmates are moving forward with jobs, careers, and stability, while I’m sitting here trying to understand my own mind. I try not to compare myself to others, but it’s difficult not to notice the difference.

When I search about schizophrenia or psychosis online, it scares me. People describe it as a serious illness, and that makes me wonder what kind of future I will have. There are moments where I wish I had been “normal.” I look back at my younger self—especially before class 9—and I remember feeling more free, more clear-minded, more connected to life.

There are also pressures that come with trying to navigate life as a Muslim in India. Sometimes it feels like you are already carrying invisible weight before life even begins testing you personally. When mental illness is added on top of that, everything becomes even harder to explain to other people.

More than anything, I wish I could go back and experience college with a healthier mind and better clarity. Maybe things would have been different. Maybe I would have built friendships more easily, focused better, and entered adulthood with confidence instead of confusion. But life doesn’t move backwards. Right now, all I can do is try to understand myself honestly and hope that even after everything, there is still some kind of future waiting for me.
