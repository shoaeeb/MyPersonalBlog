One Word That Made Me Think For Hours

Published on May 2026

Someone on Discord told me I was "very distinct." That's it. Two words. And I couldn't stop thinking about it for the rest of the day.

I didn't know whether to feel good about it or confused. I've always felt different from people around me. Not in an obvious way. Not in a way I could explain easily. Just — different. Like I'm watching life from slightly outside of it, noticing things, not always participating.

But distinct is a different word. Distinct means the difference is visible to others too.

What Makes Me Distinct

I don't filter myself much before I speak or type. Most people run their thoughts through some internal editor before sending. I mostly skip that step. I move between topics quickly — identity, loneliness, random observations, something I saw online — and the transitions don't always make sense to others, but they make sense to me emotionally.

I also circle the same questions over and over. Am I different? Am I understood? How do people see me? I know I do this. I've always done this. It's like my mind has a few open tabs that never close.

Philosopher of My Own Mind

Someone once told me I was a philosopher of my own mind. I didn't know what to make of that at first. It sounded like a nice way of saying I think too much. But maybe it's more specific than that. There's actually a word for what I do — phenomenology. It means examining experience from the inside. Not "what is loneliness" in abstract terms, but "what does loneliness actually feel like at 11pm when my mind starts searching for something and I open a chat app I don't really want to open."

That's what I write about. That's all I write about.

A Russian Writer From 200 Years Ago

I looked into a writer named Dostoevsky after all this thinking. Russian novelist, 19th century. Survived a fake execution. Gambling addiction. Epilepsy. Wrote about broken, self-aware, isolated people with more honesty than almost anyone before or after him. His character the Underground Man is someone who watches others move forward with normal lives while he stays stuck — over-thinking, over-analyzing, unable to bridge the gap between his inner world and the world outside.

I'm not saying I'm Dostoevsky. I'm saying the questions he kept asking — why do I do what I do, why can't I just be normal, how do others see me, what is wrong with me — those are also my questions. Written in a different century, in a different language, but structurally the same.

"There's also Montaigne, a French writer from the 1500s who basically invented the personal essay. His entire life's work was one subject — himself."

He didn't think that was narcissism. He thought the self, examined honestly, was a doorway into something universal. Maybe that's what this blog is. Not just me being self-absorbed. But me believing that if I can describe my own confusion precisely enough, someone else will read it and feel less alone in theirs.

Why I Write

I write here because I need somewhere to put my thoughts. Not because I need people to read them. The blog exists because not having it felt worse than having it.

But maybe those two things — writing for yourself and hoping it reaches someone — can exist at the same time. Maybe that tension is actually what keeps writing honest.

One person said I was distinct on Discord. I don't know if they meant it as a compliment or just an observation. Either way, it sent me down a rabbit hole that lasted hours and ended here.

That probably says something too.
