The Person I Could Not Stop Talking To

Published on May 27, 2026

I am not proud of this. But I write about what is real, so here it is.

I joined a Discord server ten days ago. I did not fully understand how it worked. I used my real name — Shoaeeb Osman. I met someone. I thought they were a boy at first, interacted normally, and then found out they were a girl. She said "I like you" early on. That stayed with me.

Then I got banned for breaking some rules. And instead of leaving, I came back.

Five Accounts

I created a new account. Then another. Then another. Five accounts total across ten days. Each time she recognized me. Each time I was banned again.

She told me she would not talk to me. I stayed active when she was active. She blocked me. I came back anyway. At some point she said — "idc, you were always there, can't avoid you." I held onto that like it meant something good. Looking back, it did not. It meant she was exhausted.

"Each time she recognized me and said — it is very distinct. I thought that was a compliment. Maybe it was just tiredness."

Why I Did Not Stop

I have been thinking about this honestly. Not to excuse it. Just to understand it.

I am lonely. Not just sometimes — consistently, deeply lonely. I have no friends offline. I live quietly with my family. When someone shows me even small warmth — "I like you" from a stranger on the internet — something in me holds onto it very tightly. Too tightly.

The loneliness was driving the persistence. Not malice. Not obsession for its own sake. Just — I found one small moment of warmth and I did not know how to let it go even when it was clearly gone.

That does not make what I did okay. She drew a boundary. Multiple times. In multiple ways. I kept crossing it. Whatever my reasons were — her experience was someone who would not leave her alone no matter what she did. That is not okay. I know that now more clearly than I did then.

What I Actually Learned

Loneliness, when it gets intense enough, stops being just a feeling. It starts making decisions. It tells you that one more message, one more account, one more try — maybe this time it will be different. It will not be different. The other person's boundary does not change because your need is real.

I also learned something about myself I did not want to see. I have more intensity than most people are ready for. What feels like normal attention to me can feel overwhelming to someone else. That gap — between how I experience something and how the other person experiences it — that is something I need to understand better.

I am not a bad person. But I did something that hurt someone. Both things are true.

I do not know what the right amount of connection looks like for me yet. But I know it does not start with five accounts and someone who is trying to get away.
