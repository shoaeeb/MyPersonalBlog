The Opportunity I Couldn’t Take

Published on February 11, 2026

Recently, I had an opportunity that could have changed my life. I received an offer for a sales position at Bin Dawood supermarket in Saudi Arabia. For many people, this would be exciting — a chance to work abroad, earn money, and start fresh in a new country. For me, it became something else entirely: a test I didn’t feel strong enough to face.

The journey itself felt overwhelming. I would have had to travel alone to different places — first to Bihar for a skill test, then to Mumbai, then to an office there, and later to the airport again for an international flight. To most people, this might sound normal. To me, it felt like climbing a mountain without knowing how to walk.

Fear That Doesn’t Make Sense — But Feels Real

I have been struggling with my mental health, and because of that, simple things don’t always feel simple. Going alone to unknown places brings intense fear, confusion, and doubt. I kept thinking: What if I get lost? What if I can’t manage things alone? What if something goes wrong? These thoughts kept getting louder until they drowned out the opportunity itself.

"Sometimes the hardest journeys are not across countries, but inside our own minds."

In the end, I rejected the offer. Just like that, the chance was gone. No dramatic moment. No big failure scene. Just fear quietly winning.

Feeling Left Behind

What hurts more is comparison. People my age — my batchmates — are working, earning, moving forward. Meanwhile, I struggle with basic adult things like traveling alone or making big decisions. It makes me feel like a failure. Like everyone else received a manual for life that I somehow missed.

I keep asking myself what is wrong with me. Why can’t I just be “normal”? Why do opportunities feel like threats instead of chances?

Trying to Understand, Not Just Blame

Maybe this isn’t about laziness or lack of ambition. Maybe it’s fear, anxiety, and a mind that gets overwhelmed easily. Knowing that doesn’t erase the regret, but it does change how I look at myself. Instead of seeing only failure, I’m trying to see someone who is struggling — and still trying to understand why.

I don’t know what the next opportunity will be, or whether I’ll be brave enough when it comes. But I do know this: my story isn’t over just because I missed one chance. Even if progress is slow, even if it looks different from others, I’m still here — and maybe that counts for something.
